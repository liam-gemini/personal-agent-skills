# How to Ask Good Questions — Knowledge Notes

## Source

- Author: Julia Evans
- Published: 2016-12-31
- Original: [How to ask good questions](https://jvns.ca/blog/good-questions/)
- Form: Structured summary and operational interpretation; not a reproduction of the article.

## Central idea

A good question helps another person efficiently transfer the specific knowledge the questioner wants. These techniques are aids, not admission requirements: asking basic, imperfect, or insufficiently researched questions remains legitimate.

## Core techniques

### State the current model

Describe what you currently believe, the reasoning that connects the parts, and the point of uncertainty. This lets the answerer correct the exact broken assumption instead of guessing your level or repeating background you already know.

### Seek factual answers

Replace a broad topic request with bounded questions whose answers can be checked. A precise question may reveal that the answerer does not know; that is still useful because both sides understand the unknown.

### Name confusion immediately

Stop at unfamiliar terminology or an unexplained relationship. Build a small glossary or concept map when entering a domain with many new terms. Pretending to understand only compounds later confusion.

### Research lightly, not defensively

Brief research can reveal vocabulary, candidate mechanisms, and better hypotheses. It should improve the question, not become a gate that delays all requests for help.

### Choose the audience and channel

Consider who has enough context, whether the timing is reasonable, the expected answer cost, and whether a quick message or scheduled conversation fits. The most senior expert is not always the best person to ask.

### Expose tacit knowledge

Questions about signals, judgments, and decision cues reveal expert heuristics that may not be documented. Asking an apparently basic question can make hidden assumptions visible for an entire team.

### Treat questions as contributions

Questions identify undocumented knowledge and can benefit others. Answering questions also consolidates recently learned material, creating a reinforcing learning loop.

## Construction checklist

1. What outcome do I need from the answer?
2. What do I currently believe?
3. Which exact claim, term, relationship, or decision is uncertain?
4. Can the question be answered with a fact or a correction to a model?
5. Which context would materially change the answer?
6. Who can answer with reasonable cost, and what channel fits?
7. Does the question preserve honest uncertainty without apology or noise?

## Boundary

Do not optimize wording so aggressively that asking becomes intimidating. If the user needs help now, produce the best available question and clearly mark the missing context rather than withholding assistance.
